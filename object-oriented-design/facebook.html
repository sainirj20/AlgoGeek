OBJECT ORIENTED DESIGN

Design Facebook - A Social Network

Every social network needs a mutual Friendship, and Facebook's is the same symmetric, mutual-consent shape as the Connection modeled on the LinkedIn page in this section: a request sent, a request accepted, one canonical fact true for both parties. That part of the model is settled ground and doesn't need relitigating here. What actually distinguishes Facebook's object model is a harder, far more pervasive question: who is allowed to see this? A Post isn't just content — it's content plus a configurable Audience (Public, Friends, Friends-except-someone, a Custom list, or Only-me), and that Audience has to be consulted correctly on every single read path: assembling a News Feed, rendering someone's Timeline, opening a Photo. Treat Visibility/Audience as a first-class policy object — not a bolted-on enum field — and the rest of the design (Groups with their own scoped visibility, Pages that are effectively always Public, a Block that has to override everything else, tagging that can leak a Photo beyond its Post's original audience) falls out as instances of that one rule being applied consistently everywhere content is read.

Problem Statement

Design the core object model for a general-purpose social network. Each Member builds a personal network of mutual Friendships — formed only through a FriendRequest that the other party explicitly accepts, the same symmetric shape as a Connection elsewhere in this section. Members post updates (Post, optionally carrying a Photo or video) that other Members can Like/React to and Comment on, join member-managed Groups, and Follow public Pages (a one-directional subscription, unlike Friendship). The hard part of this problem isn't any single class — it's that almost every one of those classes needs a configurable Audience/Visibility (Public, Friends, Friends-except, a Custom list, or Only-me), and that Audience has to be correctly enforced on every read path: a Member's News Feed, another Member's Timeline, a Photo Album. Interviewers use this problem to see whether a candidate treats Visibility as a first-class, pluggable policy object with its own isVisibleTo() check — one that can consult a CustomFriendList or a Group's membership — rather than a plain enum field that can't express any of that; whether Groups and Pages are modeled as structurally distinct from a personal Friendship even though all three are "network" relationships; and whether a Block is correctly treated as an absolute override that suppresses visibility bidirectionally, ahead of every other rule.

Requirements

  1. Two Members become Friends only via a mutual FriendRequest-then-Accept flow — a symmetric relationship, the same shape as a Connection elsewhere in this section, so we won't re-elaborate that distinction here.
  2. A Member can create a Post (text, Photo, or video) and must set a Visibility/Audience for it: Public, Friends, Friends-except-a-list, a Custom list, or Only-me.
  3. Other Members can Like/React to and Comment on a Post, but only if that Post's Audience/Visibility rule allows them to see it in the first place.
  4. A Member can join Groups (member-managed communities, potentially with their own posts, their own membership-approval flow, and their own per-group visibility) and Follow Pages (public figures/brands, one-directional, no mutual consent required — contrast this with Friendship).
  5. Every content read-path — News Feed, a Member's Timeline/profile wall, a Photo Album — must apply the visibility rule so a viewer only ever sees content they're authorized to see.
  6. Members receive Notifications for relevant events (a friend request, a comment on your Post, being tagged in a Photo) and can configure notification preferences.
  7. A Member can Block another Member, which must suppress all visibility and interaction bidirectionally, regardless of any Friendship/Group/Page relationship the two might otherwise share.

Actors & Use Cases

Four actors touch the system, and — as with the other case studies in this section — Member dominates the use-case count while every other actor still owns at least one use case none of the others can initiate:

  • Member — sends/accepts Friend Requests, creates Posts and sets their Audience, Likes/Reacts/Comments (subject to visibility), joins Groups, follows Pages, views their News Feed, and can Block another Member.
  • Group Admin/Moderator — approves pending Group membership requests and moderates posts inside the Group; never touches a Member's personal Friendship graph or Timeline.
  • Page Admin — posts as the Page itself, since a Page has no personal owner voice of its own; never initiates a Friendship or a Follow.
  • System — assembles each Member's News Feed by pulling candidate Posts from Friends, Groups, and followed Pages and filtering every one of them through the visibility rule before anything is shown; also dispatches Notifications. The only actor that never represents a single human decision.

Primary use cases: Send/Accept Friend Request, Create Post with Audience, Comment/React on Post, Join Group, Follow Page, View News Feed, and Block Member — plus Post as Page, shown below since it's Page Admin's one exclusive action.

Why Audience/Visibility is modeled as its own class, not a plain enum on Post, is the central decision on this page. An enum can tell you which visibility level a Post has, but it cannot evaluate anything — and evaluating visibility is exactly what the system needs to do, correctly, on every read path. Deciding whether a given Viewer can see a FriendsAudience Post requires querying the Friendship graph; deciding whether they can see a CustomListAudience Post requires checking a specific CustomFriendList's current membership; deciding whether they can see a Group post requires checking that Group's membership instead of the post's nominal audience at all. That's real behavior with real dependencies (a repository, a list, a membership check), not a static label — which is exactly the textbook justification for Strategy/Policy: give Audience a single method, isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post), and let each concrete variant implement it however it needs to, including consulting other objects the enum could never reach.

Design Patterns Applied

Name the pattern and the forcing function together, not just the label:

  • Strategy / Policy (Visibility/Audience as a pluggable policy object with an isVisibleTo() check) — the central pattern on this page. Audience is an interface; PublicAudience, FriendsAudience, CustomListAudience, and OnlyMeAudience are interchangeable strategies a Post holds one of. VisibilityPolicy (and every read path that uses it) never needs a switch on visibility type — it just calls audience.isVisibleTo(viewer, post) and trusts the concrete strategy to know how to answer, including reaching into a CustomFriendList or a Group's membership when it needs to.
  • Composite-ish sub-community (Group) — a Group behaves like a bounded, self-contained social graph: it has its own membership-approval flow and its own default Audience for the Posts made inside it, structurally similar to a personal Timeline but deliberately distinct from one. This is why Group-post visibility is handled as its own check in VisibilityPolicy rather than being folded into the ordinary Public/Friends/Custom/Only-me Audience family — see the Extensibility section below.
  • Observer (Notifications) — a new FriendRequest, a Comment on your Post, or being tagged in a Photo each publish an event that a Notification subscriber picks up and fans out (push, email, in-app badge), without the Post/Comment/Photo class needing to know how notifications are actually delivered.
  • Symmetric vs. directional relationships (Friendship vs. PageFollow) — Friendship is one canonical, undirected record; PageFollow is a directional edge with no consent step. This duality is the same one explored in full depth on the LinkedIn page in this section (Connection vs. Follow); we deliberately don't re-derive it here beyond noting that Facebook's Friendship/PageFollow pair follows the identical reasoning.

Code Skeleton

Fields and method signatures only — not a runnable program. FriendsAudience and CustomListAudience are worked out in full, since the Audience/Visibility check is exactly what interviewers probe on, and VisibilityPolicy shows the Block short-circuit that has to run ahead of every Audience check.

public class Member {
    private final String memberId;
    private String name;
    private String email;
    private MemberStatus status;

    public Member(String memberId, String name, String email) {
        this.memberId = memberId;
        this.name = name;
        this.email = email;
        this.status = MemberStatus.ACTIVE;
    }

    public String getMemberId() { return memberId; }
}
// A single record represents the (undirected) fact that two Members are friends --
// same idiom as Connection on the LinkedIn page: canonical ordering guarantees
// (A, B) and (B, A) never produce two rows.
public final class Friendship {
    private final String friendshipId;
    private final String memberIdA;
    private final String memberIdB;
    private final Instant since;

    private Friendship(String friendshipId, String memberIdA, String memberIdB, Instant since) {
        if (memberIdA.compareTo(memberIdB) <= 0) {
            this.memberIdA = memberIdA;
            this.memberIdB = memberIdB;
        } else {
            this.memberIdA = memberIdB;
            this.memberIdB = memberIdA;
        }
        this.friendshipId = friendshipId;
        this.since = since;
    }

    public static Friendship of(String friendshipId, String memberIdA, String memberIdB, Instant since) {
        return new Friendship(friendshipId, memberIdA, memberIdB, since);
    }

    public boolean involves(String memberId) {
        return memberIdA.equals(memberId) || memberIdB.equals(memberId);
    }

    public String other(String memberId) {
        if (memberId.equals(memberIdA)) return memberIdB;
        if (memberId.equals(memberIdB)) return memberIdA;
        throw new IllegalArgumentException("member is not part of this friendship");
    }
}
public class Post {
    private final String postId;
    private final String authorId;
    private String content;
    private final Instant createdAt;
    private final String groupId;   // nullable -- set only for a Group post
    private final String pageId;    // nullable -- set only for a Page post
    private Audience audience;      // ignored for Group posts; Group membership decides instead
    private final List<Comment> comments = new ArrayList<>();
    private final List<Reaction> reactions = new ArrayList<>();

    public Post(String postId, String authorId, String content, Instant createdAt,
                String groupId, String pageId, Audience audience) {
        this.postId = postId;
        this.authorId = authorId;
        this.content = content;
        this.createdAt = createdAt;
        this.groupId = groupId;
        this.pageId = pageId;
        this.audience = audience;
    }

    public void addComment(Comment comment) { comments.add(comment); }
    public void addReaction(Reaction reaction) { reactions.add(reaction); }

    public String getAuthorId() { return authorId; }
    public String getGroupId() { return groupId; }
    public Audience getAudience() { return audience; }
}
// The pluggable Visibility policy: a bare enum could tell you WHICH level a Post
// has, but couldn't evaluate anything. isVisibleTo() is the whole point.
public interface Audience {
    boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post);
}

public final class PublicAudience implements Audience {
    @Override
    public boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post) {
        return true; // anyone, including a logged-out visitor in a fuller model
    }
}

public final class OnlyMeAudience implements Audience {
    @Override
    public boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post) {
        return viewer.getMemberId().equals(post.getAuthorId());
    }
}
public final class FriendsAudience implements Audience {
    private final FriendshipRepository friendshipRepository;

    public FriendsAudience(FriendshipRepository friendshipRepository) {
        this.friendshipRepository = friendshipRepository;
    }

    @Override
    public boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post) {
        if (viewer.getMemberId().equals(post.getAuthorId())) {
            return true; // authors can always see their own post
        }
        return friendshipRepository.areFriends(viewer.getMemberId(), post.getAuthorId());
    }
}

public final class CustomListAudience implements Audience {
    private final CustomFriendList allowedList;

    public CustomListAudience(CustomFriendList allowedList) {
        this.allowedList = allowedList;
    }

    @Override
    public boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post) {
        if (viewer.getMemberId().equals(post.getAuthorId())) {
            return true;
        }
        return allowedList.contains(viewer.getMemberId());
    }
}
// Orchestrates the read-path check used by Feed, Timeline, and Photo views alike.
// The Block check runs first and short-circuits every Audience variant equally --
// even a PublicAudience post must stay hidden from a blocked party.
public class VisibilityPolicy {
    private final BlockRepository blockRepository;
    private final GroupMembershipRepository groupMembershipRepository;

    public VisibilityPolicy(BlockRepository blockRepository,
                             GroupMembershipRepository groupMembershipRepository) {
        this.blockRepository = blockRepository;
        this.groupMembershipRepository = groupMembershipRepository;
    }

    public boolean isVisibleTo(Member viewer, Post post) {
        if (blockRepository.isBlockedEitherWay(viewer.getMemberId(), post.getAuthorId())) {
            return false;
        }
        // A Group post's visibility is the Group's membership, full stop --
        // it deliberately ignores whatever personal default Audience the author has.
        if (post.getGroupId() != null) {
            return groupMembershipRepository.isMember(viewer.getMemberId(), post.getGroupId());
        }
        return post.getAudience().isVisibleTo(viewer, post);
    }
}

Extensibility & Edge Cases

  • Unfriending strips Friends-audience visibility immediately. The moment a Friendship record is removed, every FriendsAudience Post that Friendship used to unlock must become invisible on the very next read — because visibility is derived live from FriendshipRepository.areFriends() rather than cached on the Post, this falls out for free. There's no stale "was visible" flag to clean up anywhere.
  • A CustomFriendList can go stale relative to the Friendship graph. If a Member is removed from a CustomFriendList but is still a Friend, or vice versa, CustomListAudience has to check the list's current membership at read time (as shown above), not a snapshot taken when the Post was created — otherwise a Post could keep leaking to someone who was quietly dropped from "Close Friends" months ago.
  • Group posts have their own audience scope, distinct from the poster's personal default. Even if a Member's personal default Audience is Public, a Post made inside a Group is visible only to that Group's members — VisibilityPolicy checks groupId first and, if present, ignores the Post's own audience field entirely. This is exactly why the design routes Group visibility through a separate membership check rather than trying to force it into one more Audience variant.
  • Photo tagging can expose a Photo beyond its Post's original audience. If Member B tags Member C in a Photo that was posted with a Friends-of-B audience, C (and by extension C's own Friends, depending on tag-visibility settings) may now be able to see that Photo even though they were never in B's original audience. This is why Photo carries its own optional audienceOverride rather than blindly inheriting the Post's Audience unconditionally — a tag is effectively a second, additive grant of visibility that needs the tagged Member's implicit consent to exist at all.
  • Page posts are effectively always Public. Because a Page has no Friends and no Only-me concept — it's a broadcast entity by design — Page.postAsPage() constructs its Posts with a PublicAudience by convention rather than exposing the full Audience picker a Member gets; there's no meaningful "Friends" set to restrict a Page post to.

Follow-up Interview Questions

Why should Visibility/Audience be modeled as its own policy class instead of a plain enum on Post?
An enum can only ever be a static label — it can tell you a Post is "FRIENDS" or "CUSTOM," but it cannot itself answer "can this specific Viewer see this specific Post?" Answering that question correctly requires behavior with real dependencies: a FriendsAudience needs to query the Friendship graph, a CustomListAudience needs to check a specific CustomFriendList's current membership, and a Group post needs to check Group membership instead. None of that logic has anywhere to live on a bare enum constant. Modeling Audience as an interface with an isVisibleTo(viewer, post) method (the Strategy/Policy pattern) gives each visibility rule a real home for its own evaluation logic, and lets new rules (e.g., a future "Friends of Friends" audience) be added as one new class with zero changes to Post, VisibilityPolicy, or any existing Audience implementation.
How does a Block have to override every other visibility rule, even for a Public post?
A Block is a suppression rule, not "one more relationship type" layered alongside Friendship/Group/Page — it has to be checked before any Audience is ever consulted, and it has to return "not visible" unconditionally regardless of what that Audience would otherwise say. Concretely, VisibilityPolicy.isVisibleTo() checks BlockRepository.isBlockedEitherWay(viewer, author) as its very first line and returns false immediately if it's true, never even calling into post.getAudience().isVisibleTo(...). This is deliberate: a PublicAudience post is, by definition, visible to literally everyone except the people a Block explicitly carves out — if the Block check ran second, or was implemented as just another Audience variant that a bare PublicAudience could "outrank," a blocked Member could still see a blocker's Public content, which defeats the entire point of blocking someone.
How do you keep a Group's post visibility distinct from a Member's personal default Audience?
Give Post a nullable groupId, and have VisibilityPolicy branch on it before ever looking at the Post's own audience field: if groupId != null, visibility is entirely "is this Viewer a member of that Group?" via GroupMembership, full stop — the author's personal default Audience (even if it's Public) is simply never consulted for a Group post. This mirrors real product behavior: a Post made inside a private Group shouldn't leak to the author's Public-by-default Timeline audience just because that's their usual setting. Keeping the branch explicit in VisibilityPolicy, rather than trying to shoehorn "Group members only" into one more Audience implementation, keeps the two visibility systems (personal Audience vs. Group membership) from silently interacting in surprising ways.
Where's the line between this page's OOD-level visibility filter and an HLD-level feed-ranking system?
OOD scope stops at correctly determining which candidate Posts a Viewer is even authorized to see — the Block check, then the Audience/Group-membership check, expressed as the classes and method signatures shown above. The moment the conversation turns to how those already-visible candidates get ordered — engagement-based ranking scores, fan-out-on-write vs. fan-out-on-read, caching layers, sharding the social graph, handling a Page with tens of millions of followers — that's a distributed-systems/HLD conversation, exactly the same boundary the LinkedIn page in this section draws for its own Feed sequence diagram. A strong candidate names this boundary explicitly rather than drifting into fan-out architecture on a question meant to test visibility correctness.
What has to happen the instant two Members unfriend each other?
The Friendship record is deleted (or flagged inactive), and because FriendsAudience and CustomListAudience both evaluate membership live — querying FriendshipRepository/CustomFriendList at read time rather than trusting a cached "visible" flag on the Post — every FriendsAudience Post the removed Friendship used to unlock becomes invisible on the very next read, with no separate cleanup step required. The one thing worth calling out: if the unfriended Member was also in a CustomFriendList the author maintains, that list itself doesn't automatically get updated just because the Friendship ended — that's the stale-CustomFriendList edge case, a distinct failure mode from unfriending itself.
Why can a CustomFriendList silently drift out of sync with the Friendship graph, and how should CustomListAudience handle that?
CustomFriendList is a separate, independently-maintained collection of memberIds — there's no enforced invariant tying its contents to who is currently a Friend, so a Member could remain on a "Close Friends" list long after being unfriended, or a brand-new Friend might not yet have been added to any list. CustomListAudience has to treat the list's membership as the sole source of truth at evaluation time (calling allowedList.contains(viewerId) live, as shown in the code skeleton) rather than assuming list membership implies current Friendship, or vice versa — the two are related concepts a product might want to reconcile with a UI nudge, but they are not the same data and shouldn't be treated as interchangeable in the visibility check itself.
Why is a Page's Follow directional while a Friendship is symmetric, and why can't a Page have a Friendship?
Friendship presumes two peers who both explicitly consent to a mutual bond — the entire FriendRequest/Accept flow exists to capture that mutual consent. A Page is a broadcast entity, not a peer: there's no meaningful sense in which a Page "consents" to a relationship with a Member the way another Member would. PageFollow correctly captures what following a Page actually is — a one-directional subscription to its updates, with no consent step and no expectation of reciprocity — which is precisely the same Follow-vs-Connection duality explored on the LinkedIn page in this section, just with Page standing in for Company.
Walk through the Photo-tagging edge case: how can a tag expose a Photo beyond the audience its Post was originally set to?
Suppose Member B posts a Photo with a FriendsAudience, then tags Member C, who is not B's Friend (say, a coworker B isn't connected to but happened to be in the picture with). If Photo simply inherited the Post's Audience unconditionally, C would have no path to ever see a Photo they're literally in. Modeling Photo with its own optional audienceOverride lets tagging grant additional, narrowly-scoped visibility — typically "the tagged Member (and optionally their own audience, subject to product policy) can see this specific Photo" — without loosening the Post's overall Audience for everyone else. The key modeling point: a tag is an additive grant tied to the tagged Member's implicit consent to being identified, not a blanket loosening of the original Post's visibility rule.
Why does Commenting/Reacting need to re-check visibility instead of trusting that the Post was already shown to the Viewer once?
Visibility isn't a one-time gate checked only when a Post first renders in a Feed — it has to hold at the moment of every subsequent interaction too, because the underlying facts it depends on can change between the two moments. A Friendship could be dissolved, a Block could be created, or a Member could be removed from a CustomFriendList in the window between "the Post rendered in my Feed" and "I click Comment." Re-running VisibilityPolicy.isVisibleTo(viewer, post) as a precondition before accepting a new Comment or Reaction (not just before rendering the Post) closes that gap; trusting a stale "I already saw this once" assumption would let a since-blocked or since-unfriended Member keep commenting on content they should no longer have any access to at all.

Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways
  • Visibility/Audience is a first-class policy object, not a bolted-on enum — it needs an isVisibleTo(viewer, post) method that can query a Friendship graph, a CustomFriendList, or a Group's membership, none of which a bare enum constant can do.
  • Friendship is the same symmetric, mutual-consent shape as Connection on the LinkedIn page — settled ground, deliberately not re-elaborated here so the page can focus on visibility.
  • A Block is a suppression rule that must be checked before any Audience is consulted, and it overrides every other rule unconditionally — even a PublicAudience post must stay hidden from a blocked party.
  • Group posts route visibility through Group membership, deliberately ignoring the author's personal default Audience — a private Group shouldn't leak content just because the poster's usual setting is Public.
  • Every read path — Feed, Timeline, Photo view — must apply the exact same VisibilityPolicy check; the Feed's job, per this page's OOD scope, is producing the correctly-filtered candidate set, not ranking or ordering it (that's HLD scope, mirroring the same boundary drawn on the LinkedIn page).
